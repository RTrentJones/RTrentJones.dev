> Polyphony is a multi-user writing app for novels. An author adds their own material, and
> Polyphony proposes the book's canon from it: the cast, the facts of the world, a style guide and a
> synopsis. The author reviews and commits that canon. Every outline, scene and revision the model
> writes afterwards is built from it and checked against it. It is invite-only, runs on free-tier
> models by default, and is in the middle of a long evaluation effort, which this page describes as
> it stands.

## The incident that shaped it

Polyphony's requirements document opens with a failure. An author supplied a 20,001-character
synopsis for a satirical novel about a lich systems analyst, an uncontrollable sorceress and a
four-hundred-year-old Chief Executive Lich. Polyphony returned a twelve-chapter outline starring
"Elara", a character who appears nowhere in the book. It read "the CEL" as a company called
"Corporeal Energy Logistics", left out the entire cast, and turned a quiet story about consent into
a chosen-one rebellion. The author's note on the output reads "THIS IS CLEARLY NOTHING LIKE MY
DESCRIPTION."

The cause wasn't the prompt. A sanitizer cut the synopsis to its first 2,000 characters, a filter
meant to block prompt injection rewrote every `--` in the text, and the character list came back
empty because a foreign key was never written.

Everything since follows from that. The book is the root of every concept in the data model,
extraction proposes and the author commits, the outline has to pass a check that the book's
principal cast appears in it, and the author's text reaches the model as quoted story material
instead of being filtered.

## What it does today

- **Sources.** An author creates a book and adds sources, uploaded files or pasted text.
- **Canon.** Polyphony extracts a proposed cast, world facts, a style guide and a synopsis, which
  the author reviews and commits. Entities are versioned, so earlier versions can be restored.
- **Voices.** Each character's lines are indexed, and scene generation draws on samples of how
  that character speaks.
- **Outline.** A staged outline runs as a background job: the premise is restated for the author to
  confirm, then chapters and beats are planned, with a hard check that the principal cast appears.
- **Scenes.** Outline chapters become chapters with planned beats, and scenes are generated one at
  a time or for a whole chapter.
- **Revision and continuity.** Revisions arrive as proposals the author accepts or discards, and
  continuity findings quote the passage they refer to.
- **Export.** A book exports as a manuscript, by chapter range.

## How it's built

Polyphony is one FastAPI application serving a statically exported Next.js frontend from a single
container on an Oracle Cloud free-tier instance, behind a Cloudflare tunnel. One Neon Postgres
database holds both the book data and the voice index, using pgvector, and embeddings are computed
locally with fastembed.

The model layer is a registry of providers chosen by configuration. By default every account writes
with Gemini Flash-Lite on its free tier, paced to its rate limits. An allowlisted account can be
routed to Claude, and a monthly spend ceiling is checked before every paid call; a call that would
cross it pauses the job instead of failing it. Background jobs pause the same way on quota limits,
and the worker sits idle without querying the database, so Neon can suspend it between uses.
Registration needs an invite code, because every account shares the same model quota.

## How it's evaluated

The evaluations use public-domain novels as labelled data: *Frankenstein*, *Dracula* and *Wuthering
Heights*, with every character and place renamed. There is also a synthetic book written to
reproduce the Elara failure. A finished novel provides a true cast, each character's real dialogue,
a plot and a timeline, so the pipeline's output can be scored against it. Renaming removes the
easiest cue but doesn't stop a model from recognising famous prose. Because the prompts have been
tuned against those books for many rounds, two further novels are held out and run only once per
release.

The measures cover extraction against the true cast, retrieval quality, whether an outline keeps
the book's cast, whether continuity checks catch errors planted in a chapter, and the quality of the
prose. The prose is judged by a model from a different family than the one that wrote it. In CI, an
outline of the synthetic book has to keep at least 90% of its principal cast.

Most of the recent work has gone into making those measurements trustworthy before relying on them
for product decisions. In late October that meant a fixed $64 budget spent on a series of
experiments:

- Claude Sonnet as a judge caught every visible kind of damage planted in test chapters, including
  a removed plot change that the free judge mostly missed. Its scores also varied little between
  two passes over the same chapters.
- A narrator test that the free judge had failed turned out to be the judge's limitation, not the
  test's. A redesigned version picks the right narrator with both judges: every time with Sonnet,
  and three times in four with the free judge.
- An earlier comparison couldn't tell whether retrieving each character's voice samples writes
  better scenes than a simpler approach, which quotes each character's first few lines. With the
  stronger judge it remains a near tie, 0.56 with a 95% interval of 0.36 to 0.75 over 18 scenes. A
  paid multi-model ensemble came last in every comparison.
- The main question, whether the newest release writes better chapters than the one before, is
  being measured blind on whole chapters through November 10. Its judgments stay sealed until
  generation ends, under a plan signed before any data was produced.

## Where it stands

Polyphony works end to end for invited accounts, and it is still experimental. Ensemble writing is
switched off on the free tier, accessibility work is partly done, and the evaluation above found
that an outline can plan chapter beats that name no character, which generation then refuses. That
defect is queued for a later release.

The status link on this page goes to Polyphony's signals on [Tracer](/tracer/), which currently
show red. Those runs come from the experiments described above, which collect judgments for
separate analysis instead of scoring cases, and Polyphony's eval report marks any run with no scored
cases as failed. For the chapter comparison, the judgments stay sealed until November 10 by design.
